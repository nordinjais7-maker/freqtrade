# Trade forced flow, not order-book folklore

None of the seven strategy families has credible, after-cost evidence of a retail-tradable intraday edge in BTC or SUI USDT-margined perps. One effect has rigorous-looking support, and it is not a "liquidity hunt". After a short move driven by aggressive taker flow, the move tends to partially reverse over roughly 15 minutes to a few hours. A 2026 preprint finds this in **90% of 183 Binance pairs**. It reads the effect as a premium for providing liquidity, so it is naturally earned with maker fills, and the effect is statistically small ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)). The rest of the evidence is mostly negative. Stop-triggered moves through levels *continue* for hours, which is why sweep fades fail ([NY Fed SR150](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fip:fednsr:150)). Order-flow and book-imbalance predictability lasts seconds and loses to fees even for passive quoting ([arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18625)). Long/short ratios have no published out-of-sample (OOS) validation. Iceberg, absorption and liquidation-heatmap methods rest almost entirely on vendor and educator material. Your failed sweep-fade, breakout and long/short-filtered breakdown tests are what this literature predicts, not bad luck. Three things are worth testing next, in this order: (1) a maker-entry reversal after taker-flow flushes, conditioned on flow intensity; (2) a frozen replication of your 1h trend-alignment signal with funding and long/short filters, judged only on pairs and dates you have not seen; (3) a cheap falsification test of the liquidation-map "magnet" using a transparent open-interest-based clone. All three can be backtested in Freqtrade from free Binance archives. A caveat applies to everything below: arxiv.org, ssrn.com, newyorkfed.org and several exchange and vendor domains could not be reached from the research environment. Many key figures (AUCs, R² values, Sharpe ratios, fee tiers) come from search-engine snippets, not full texts, and should be checked against the papers before you rely on them.

## Thin evidence, much of it unverified or sold

The evidence falls into three tiers. Peer-reviewed or documented working papers with OOS tests are the strongest. Most of them are FX or equity studies, cited here for mechanism. Newer crypto preprints come next; several are dated 2026 and are not peer-reviewed. Last are practitioner and vendor sources. None of the full PDFs for the core crypto papers could be fetched. That includes arXiv 2608.21888 (flow-driven reversal), 2607.09426 (the quarter-hour effect), 2108.09750 (sub-second BTC trade-flow imbalance) and 2212.06888 (perpetual futures pricing). Their numbers come from abstracts and snippets, and in some cases it was not clear whether an R² was forward-looking or contemporaneous. Osler's FX stop-loss papers were also only read through abstracts and secondary summaries. So were Binance's API documentation on liquidation-stream throttling and the Tardis and Coinglass pricing pages.

The commercial share of the material is large. A rough impression from the search results is that **more than 80% of easily found material** on icebergs, absorption and liquidation heatmaps comes from vendors, educators, or media relaying vendor charts. That figure is not measured. Coinglass, Hyblock, Kingfisher, Bookmap, Exocharts and TradingLite all sell products whose value depends on the signal being seen as useful, and none publishes out-of-sample hit rates. Kingfisher's "85.8% report better outcomes" is a user-satisfaction claim with no methodology ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/the-kingfisher/reviews)). Survivorship bias affects both the strategy literature and your universe. Practitioner charts show the levels that got hit. SUI is studied because it is listed and liquid today. Its Binance perp history starts only on **2023-05-03** ([Binance archive listing](https://s3-ap-northeast-1.amazonaws.com/data.binance.vision?prefix=data/futures/um/daily/metrics/SUIUSDT/&max-keys=1)), so there are few independent cascade events and every SUI result has low statistical power. No academic study specific to SUI perps was found for any family.

## Stop cascades continue, which is why your sweep fades and crowding filters failed

The best mechanism evidence for "stop hunts" comes from FX. Osler's dealing-bank order data shows take-profit orders clustering *at* round numbers and stop-losses clustering *just beyond* them. Reaching the take-profit clusters predicts bounces. Crossing into the stop clusters predicts faster moves ([NY Fed SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)). The follow-up finds that stop-triggered moves are larger and longer-lasting than take-profit reactions. They are **significant for hours, not days**, and they sometimes come in waves ([EconPapers SR150](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fip:fednsr:150)). A sweep fade enters after the stops fire and bets against that cascade leg. In BTC, prices cluster heavily at round numbers but show **no significant pattern in returns afterwards** ([Urquhart 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176517303233)). Seven years of Binance 1-minute data show round numbers act only as *weak barriers*, with roughly equal time spent above and below them ([RIBAF 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/riibaf/v90y2026ics0275531926002217.html)). No academic test of session-range sweeps in crypto exists. Independent mechanical tests of ICT/SMC rules show the pattern your Asia fade produced: high win rates and negative expectancy. One study covering 738 assets found the sweep rule **won 71.9% of trades but returned −7.4%**, and across 903 assets it beat buy-and-hold in only **8%** of them. Trend components held up better than sweep reversals ([IndicatorEdge](https://indicatoredge.io/learn/smc-ict-strategy-backtest-evidence-win-rate)). Another daily-bar study found no statistically significant edge ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)).

Breakouts don't simply win when fades lose, and the reason is horizon. Osler's continuation effect is FX-only and fades within hours. In crypto, 15-minute moves driven by taker flow mostly *revert* ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)). A rule that buys a range break at bar close can catch the end of a flow burst that is about to reverse. Your weak London-over-New-York result fits with spot-BTC evidence that intraday momentum is strongest in high-volume, high-volatility sessions ([Shen, Urquhart & Wang 2022](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum/)). That evidence comes from spot venues, not perps.

Positioning data does no better. Searches found **no academic test** of Binance, OKX or Bybit account or top-trader long/short ratios as return predictors. These ratios count accounts, not size, and they differ by exchange ([CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/c1303-bitcoin-futures-long-short-ratios-analysis)). The closest rigorous result explains why your crowding-filtered breakdown short failed. Across 274 extreme-negative-funding events in BTC, ETH and SOL, prices reversed on up-market days and kept falling on down days, so the sign of a crowding signal depends on a market direction you can't know in advance ([Shek, SSRN 7520320](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7520320), snippet only). BitMEX's own study of fading extreme funding admits that **collecting funding provides "a significant majority" of the returns** ([BitMEX blog](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/xbtusd-funding-mean-reversion-strategy)). The robust money in funding is delta-neutral carry, with a reported **Sharpe of 1.8 for BTC under retail costs** ([He et al., arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v6), snippet figure), and that premium has been declining ([arXiv survey citing BIS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)). It is not an intraday directional edge.

## Forced-flow reversal is the only effect with cross-market support

Liquidations are a large, recurring flow, not rare tail events. One BitMEX study puts daily forced liquidations at an average of **3.51% of outstanding futures** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349159585_Liquidation_Leverage_and_Optimal_Margin_in_Bitcoin_Futures_Markets)). What happens after a flush is the important part. The 2026 Kitron & Wengrowicz preprint finds 15-minute reversal in **90% of 183 Binance pairs versus 2.7% of 187 US stocks and ETFs**, under one OOS protocol. The reversal concentrates after moves driven by aggressive taker volume and grows with flow intensity. How much order-book depth the move consumed does *not* matter, which the authors read as compensated liquidity provision ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)). Two other studies agree. Short-term reversal concentrates in smaller, less liquid, more volatile coins ([Riksbank WP 413 / JBF 2022](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2022/no.-413-trading-volume-and-liquidity-provision-in-cryptocurreny-markets.pdf)). It is amplified by liquidations in leveraged trading ([CAFR](https://www.emerald.com/cafr/article/27/4/493/1271913/Unravelling-cross-sectional-patterns-in)).

The effect is small, though. The classifier gap is an AUC of **+0.031 as designed and +0.011 (95% CI +0.008 to +0.014) under the most conservative accounting**, and the snippets did not show how costs were handled. Timing is also a constraint. In the October 2025 crash, **most forced selling happened within about 30 minutes** ([arXiv 2608.03616](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03616)), and Hyperliquid auto-deleveraging closed about **$2.1bn of positions in 12 minutes** ([arXiv 2512.01112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01112)). A 5-minute or 15-minute bar strategy that enters at the close of the flush candle will miss part of the reversal or catch the second wave. Cascades caused by outside news show no early warning in price ([arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27070)). After the largest events, market makers pulled liquidity ([CoinDesk on BitMEX report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/01/08/october-s-crypto-crash-left-market-makers-stuffed-with-coins-slowing-trading-bitmex)), so costs rise exactly when the signal fires. The practical reading: this is a liquidity-provision premium. It is worth testing only with **resting limit (maker) entries**, and SUI should show it more strongly than BTC but will also cost more to trade.

## Order-book signals decay within seconds and lose to fees, even as maker

The famous order-flow-imbalance R² of about 65% is **contemporaneous**: the imbalance explains the price change over the same window ([Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1712822)). It is not a forecast. In crypto, forward-looking fits are modest. Sub-second Bybit BTC-perp targets give an R² of roughly **0.12–0.17** ([Albers et al., arXiv 2108.09750](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.09750); horizon framing not fully confirmed). BTC order-flow-imbalance R² falls from about **0.094 at 1 second to 0.019 at 10 seconds** ([arXiv 2308.14235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14235)). In equities, lagged order-flow forecasting power is gone within about 5 minutes ([SSRN 3993561](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3993561)). The only order-imbalance effect found at horizons of a minute or more is the Binance quarter-hour opening imbalance, which predicts 4–12-hour returns out of sample. It is worth about **0.51 bp gross per trade**, roughly a twentieth of a 10 bp taker round trip ([arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/the-invisible-opening-bell-inside-cryptos-endless-trading-day/)). An independent rebuild of that signal is titled "Real Pattern, No Trade" ([Microstructure Lab](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/the-quarter-hour-clock-in-crypto)).

CVD and taker buy/sell ratios mostly re-describe the bar they come from. On BitMEX, trade-flow imbalance explains same-window price changes better than full-book imbalance ([Silantyev 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/digfin/v1y2019i1d10.1007_s42521-019-00007-w.html)). Hourly Binance taker imbalance *responds* to perp mispricing rather than forecasting returns ([arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888)). In BTC options, hourly order imbalance predicts volatility, not direction ([arXiv 2109.02776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.02776)). No academic test of "CVD divergence" exists. Toxicity measures such as VPIN point to volatility and crash risk ([Guo & Liu 2020](https://imf.nycu.edu.tw/en/30/40101/per1/Could-VPIN-Predict-Bitcoin%E2%80%99s-Crash-61643859)), and in S&P futures VPIN adds nothing once volume and volatility are controlled for ([Andersen & Bondarenko](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revfin/v19y2015i1p1-54..html)). These belong in risk and "stand aside" filters, not in entry triggers.

Making markets does not rescue book imbalance. Net-of-fee tables for naive and imbalance-aware maker quoting on crypto are **all negative**, because queue position drives adverse selection ([arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18625)). A Binance L2 replay that modelled queue and latency found every variant losing after fees. Passive quotes lost about **1.2 bp to adverse selection**, and cutting latency from 200 ms to 5 ms barely helped ([SAIndustries backtest](https://github.com/SAIndustries/LOB-Market-Making)). The main open-source tutorial assumes Binance's best maker *rebate* (−0.005%), not the retail +0.02% ([hftbacktest](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Market%20Making%20with%20Alpha%20-%20Order%20Book%20Imbalance.html)).

Icebergs, absorption and spoofing are weaker still. Reliable iceberg detection uses order-ID (market-by-order) feeds, as on CME ([Zotikov & Antonov](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.09495)). Synthetic icebergs "can only be detected heuristically" ([dxFeed](https://dxfeed.com/solutions/iceberg-detection-solution/)). Binance, Bybit and OKX publish price-level aggregated books, so an apparent refill cannot be told apart from market makers re-quoting, and there is no ground truth to validate labels. Absorption has **"no reliable published win rate"** ([TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/volume-and-order-flow/absorption)), and the same event gets called absorption before a reversal and exhaustion before a breakout. Spoofing is documented: in one Binance incident, a sell order of about 2,500 BTC vanished ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/04/29/a-vanishing-usd212m-bitcoin-order-caused-chaos-for-traders-is-spoofing-back-in-crypto)). The distance of an order from the touch is a key spoofing feature ([arXiv 2504.15908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15908v1)). That means deep-book walls, where liquidity-map narratives place their levels, are the least trustworthy displayed liquidity, especially on a thin book like SUI.

## Liquidation heatmaps are uncalibrated models, and the magnet has an obvious artefact

Coinglass and Hyblock heatmaps are not observed positions. They project open-interest changes onto assumed **25x/50x/100x leverage tiers** ([Hyblock Academy](https://academy.hyblockcapital.com/tools/liquidation-levels)). Coinglass itself says actual liquidations are "often lower" than the intensity shown ([Coinglass Learn](https://www.coinglass.com/learn/how-to-use-liqmap-to-assist-trading-en)). Hyblock's levels are "opening but not closing", so stale clusters pile up. No vendor publishes calibration or OOS accuracy, and no peer-reviewed magnet test exists. The only explicit test design found is an unfinished GitHub project. Its calibration noise band is wide: 43–55% "heavier side touched first" on random seeds, against 60–69% for a planted effect ([dejixy/analytix PR #86](https://github.com/dejixy/analytix/pull/86)). The artefact is structural. Fixed leverage tiers put clusters at roughly constant distances from recent price, so ordinary volatility touches the nearest ones no matter what. A real magnet has to beat a distance-matched touch base rate. The defensible version of the idea is different. Liquidations execute as market orders, so a move that *reaches* a real cluster can overshoot through it, which fits evidence that liquidations amplify volatility ([OECD WP 48](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/07/defi-liquidations_89cba79d/0524faaf-en.pdf)). Overshoot-after-reaching and attraction-beforehand imply opposite trades.

The ground-truth tape is also degraded. Binance's liquidation streams push only **the latest liquidation per symbol per 1000 ms** ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams/Liquidation-Order-Streams)), so any recorded or vendor liquidation history undercounts bursts ([Coin Metrics](https://gitbook-docs.coinmetrics.io/market-data/market-data-overview/liquidations/futures-liquidations)).

Binance's public archive for USDT-M perps covers both symbols. The metrics files carry open interest, top-trader and account long/short ratios, and the taker long/short volume ratio. bookDepth is cumulative depth in ±0.2% to ±5% bands, **not price-level L2**. Three gaps matter. bookTicker (top-of-book quotes) stops in 2024-04, so spreads after that can't be backtested. There is **no USDT-M liquidation dataset**. Freqtrade 2026.10 can backtest candle-level order flow from those aggTrades: footprint, delta, min/max delta and stacked imbalances. ## Pre-registered test plan for the top three

**General protocol.** Commit the specification below to git, with the hash recorded, before loading any data beyond what is needed to build features. Costs for all three candidates: maker 0.02%, taker 0.05%, and taker-leg slippage of 1 bp for BTC and 3 bp for SUI. The slippage figures are assumptions, because no sourced spread data exists after 2024-04. Funding is applied from Freqtrade's funding candles. A maker fill counts only if price trades **at least one tick through** the limit price. Freqtrade fills on touch, so check this with a short aggTrades script, or shift the backtest limit price by one tick against yourself. Report every parameter combination tried. Each candidate gets **one** OOS evaluation, with no re-tuning after it is seen. Run a Holm correction across the three candidates and two pairs when reading the p-values. A candidate that passes then goes to a **6-month forward dry-run (Nov 2026 to Apr 2027)** before any capital. The forward pass rule is: mean net per trade ≥ 0, and live maker fill rate at least 60% of the backtest-implied rate. Use the same headline metric as your earlier work, the minimum profit factor across pair-year cells, so the results are comparable with your 0.72–0.89 baseline.

### Candidate 1: maker-entry flow-flush reversal on 5m bars (BTC, SUI)

**Event.** The 15-minute return (three 5m bars) is at least *k* × its trailing 7-day standard deviation, *and* the taker share of 15-minute volume in the move's direction is at or above percentile *p* of its trailing 30-day distribution. Taker sell volume is volume minus taker-buy volume from the klines.

**Trade.** Fade the move. Place a limit order at the event close minus *e* × ATR(14, 5m) on the reversal side, valid for 15 minutes. Exits are fixed and not tuned: take profit at 50% retracement of the 15-minute move, stop 1.0 × the move's range beyond its extreme, and a 60-minute time stop. Skip entries within 10 minutes of a funding settlement.

**Grid.** *k* ∈ {2.5, 3.0}, *p* ∈ {90, 95}, *e* ∈ {0, 0.25}, which gives 8 combinations. Select on the design window **2023-05-03 to 2024-12-31** using the median of per-half-year profit factors. Evaluate once on **2025-01-01 to 2026-10-31**, a window that includes the October 2025 cascade.

**Mechanism control.** Run the same events and exits on moves of the same size whose taker share is *below* the 50th percentile. This tests the paper's claim that flow, not price, drives the reversal.

**Diagnostic only, not a gate.** Split events by whether metrics open interest fell by at least 1% over the 15 minutes, as a free proxy for liquidation-driven flushes.

| Criterion (each pair separately, OOS) | Pass threshold |
|---|---|
| Filled trades | ≥ 150 |
| Net profit factor, base cost model | ≥ 1.15 |
| Profit factor in each calendar year (2025, 2026 YTD) | ≥ 1.00 |
| Mean net per trade | ≥ +3 bp, block-bootstrap 95% CI lower bound > 0 |
| Flow-conditioned minus low-flow control, mean net | ≥ +3 bp |
| Stress: slippage doubled and 1-tick trade-through | Profit factor ≥ 1.05 |

The candidate fails if any row fails. Passing on SUI alone is reported as "SUI-only, low power", not as a general edge.

### Candidate 2: frozen trend-alignment rule with positioning filters

**Base rule.** Your 1h 4h-trend/1h-RSI alignment rule, frozen exactly as previously specified. It was found on 2023–2026 BTC/SUI data, so those dates cannot serve as a holdout.

**Filters, fixed in advance.** F1 skips longs when the latest funding rate is in the top decile of its trailing 90-day distribution, and skips shorts when it is in the bottom decile. F2 is your absolute long/short filter, replicated exactly as you used it. F2′ is a percentile version: skip longs when the account long/short ratio (`count_long_short_ratio`) is above the trailing 90-day 80th percentile, and skip shorts below the 20th. Evaluate base, base+F1, base+F2, base+F2′ and base+F1+F2.

**Holdout.** (a) Binance perps you have not used before, chosen and listed in the commit before running. **ETH is not eligible:** this exact rule (`m_trend4h_rsi1h` on `SessionMomentum1h`) was already evaluated out of sample on ETH 2024-26 (PF 1.04 / 1.05) and on BTC and SUI 2023-24 (0.72 / 0.79), and failed the 1.10 bar (see `user_data/NOTES.md` §4f). Use pairs such as SOL, XRP or DOGE instead. (b) Forward BTC and SUI data from November 2026.

**Note on priors.** Because the base rule has already failed one pre-registered out-of-sample test, this candidate tests only whether the positioning filters rescue it. The prior in the ranking table should be read as the lower end of its range.

**Pass rule.** The best filtered variant must reach a net profit factor of **≥ 1.10 in the unseen-pair sample**, with a minimum profit factor of ≥ 0.95 across pair-year cells. It must lift profit factor by **≥ 0.05 over base in at least 75% of pair-year cells**, and it must keep at least 40% of base trades. If only F2 on BTC passes, record that as a single-pair replication, not as evidence for long/short ratios in general. Shek's result predicts that this filter's sign flips with market direction.

### Candidate 3: liquidation-band clone, magnet vs overshoot (analysis before any strategy)

**Construction.** For each 5-minute metrics bar where open interest rises, assign the increase to that bar's typical price. Split it 50/50 between longs and shorts, because the true split is unknown. Project liquidation prices at 25x, 50x and 100x using Binance's maintenance-margin tier for the notional. Remove a band when price crosses it, and scale bands down when open interest falls.

**Test T1 (magnet).** Every hour, find the heaviest band within ±3% on each side of price. The outcome is which side is touched first within 4 hours. The null is a distance-matched base rate: the same distances with the sides swapped at random, 1,000 permutations.

**Test T2 (overshoot).** After price touches a top-decile band, measure the 30-minute forward return in the direction of the crossing. Compare it with touches of bands below the median in weight.

**Pass rule.** T1 passes if the heavier side is touched first at a rate that exceeds the null by **≥ 5 percentage points** with a CI excluding zero, for both pairs and in at least 3 of 4 years. T2 passes if the mean difference is **≥ 8 bp**, which is above the 7 bp maker-entry/taker-exit cost, with a block-bootstrap CI lower bound above 0, for both pairs. A strategy is designed and pre-registered separately only if at least one test passes. If both fail, stop paying for heatmap subscriptions as a trading input.

## Conclusion

Every hunting approach (sweeps, icebergs, walls, heatmap magnets) assumes the trader can see where others are trapped. Every rigorous result points the other way. The visible footprints of forced flow are either already priced within seconds or produce *continuation*, and what remains is a small premium for supplying liquidity to people who must trade. That changes the question from "where will price hunt?" to "can I be the patient counterparty at retail fees?". Only candidate 1 asks that directly, and its main enemy is execution quality, not signal discovery: maker fills that Freqtrade's touch-fill model overstates, and cascade spreads that widen exactly when it fires. If it fails, the honest conclusion is that intraday directional trading of these perps at retail costs and a 5-second loop has no documented edge. The defensible paths left would then be delta-neutral funding carry, the only family here with published after-cost returns, or investing in tick-level infrastructure and fee tiers, where the evidence says even professionals struggle without rebates.

Treat SUI as a stress test, not a primary market. Its short history, thin book and survivorship-selected status mean that a pass on SUI alone should raise your suspicion before it raises your position size.
