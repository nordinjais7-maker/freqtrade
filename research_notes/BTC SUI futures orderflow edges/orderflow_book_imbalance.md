## Q1. What do Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov (2014) and follow-ups find, and which crypto studies replicate OFI / book-imbalance predictability (horizons, R²)?

### Takeaway
OFI's famous high R² (~65%) is a **contemporaneous** relation (OFI in a window explains the price change in the *same* window), not a forecast. Crypto studies replicate the contemporaneous OFI/trade-flow-imbalance relationship on BTC perps (BitMEX, Bybit, Binance), but genuinely *forward-looking* R² is small (roughly 1–16% at sub-second to ~1 s horizons, falling toward ~2% by 10 s) and decays within seconds to a few minutes.

### Cited Findings
- **[EQUITY-MECHANISM] Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014), *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 12(1):47–88.** NYSE TAQ data for 50 US stocks: "over short time intervals, price changes are mainly driven by the order flow imbalance… at the best bid and ask"; the relation is linear with slope inversely proportional to market depth. Average R² ≈ 65% for OFI vs ≈ 32% for trade imbalance (a secondary blog quotes ~70%). — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1712822); [arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6402); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jfinec:v:12:y:2014:i:1:p:47-88.)
- Commentary on CKS stresses that the R² is measured over the same period as the imbalance ("order flow imbalances computed over a historic period explains the price change over the same period"), so it does not by itself show forecasting power. — [quantmemo summary](https://quantmemo.com/writing/paper-cont-kukanov-stoikov-order-flow-imbalance) (secondary)
- **[EQUITY-MECHANISM] Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang ("Price Impact of Order Flow Imbalance: Multi-level, Cross-sectional and Forecasting" / "Cross-Impact of Order Flow Imbalance in Equity Markets", 2021–23).** Multi-level OFI from the top 10 levels is aggregated by PCA into an "integrated OFI"; the first PC explains >89% of variance across levels, and it beats best-level OFI for contemporaneous impact. Cross-asset OFI adds nothing contemporaneously, but **lagged cross-asset OFI improves forecasts of future returns; the predictive content is significant only at short horizons (up to ~5 minutes) and decays rapidly.** A fragmentary snippet mentions an out-of-sample figure of ~1.39% for best-level OFIs (context unclear; do not quote as a firm R²). — [SSRN 3993561](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3993561); [arXiv 2112.13213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.13213v1); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Price-Impact-of-Order-Flow-Imbalances:-Multi-level,-Cont-Cucuringu/0c5915d0ff439fd0329d68612c46ef134d2c52f9)
- Note: the assignment's "Xu, Gould, Howison multi-level OFI" paper was not surfaced by search; the multi-level OFI results located were Cont–Cucuringu–Zhang. Treat the Xu–Gould–Howison specifics as a gap.
- **[EQUITY-MECHANISM] Kolm, Turiel & Westray (2023), "Deep order flow imbalance: Extracting alpha at multiple horizons from the limit order book", *Mathematical Finance* 33(4):1044–1081.** 115 Nasdaq stocks, message-level data; deep models trained on stationary **order-flow** inputs significantly outperform models trained on raw order-book states; alpha is extracted at multiple short horizons (per-horizon tables not retrieved). An independent GitHub replication on IEX data reports tiny/negative out-of-sample R² (ARX: −0.25% on order flow vs −0.52% on LOB; MLP: +0.17% vs −2.38%). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mafi.12413); [SSRN 3900141](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3900141); [replication, PRACTITIONER](https://github.com/Sudarikovamaria/DEEP-ORDER-FLOW-IMBALANCE-EXTRACTING-ALPHA-AT-MULTIPLE-HORIZONS-FROM-THE-LIMIT-ORDER-BOOK)
- **[EQUITY-MECHANISM] DeepLOB (Zhang, Zohren & Roberts, 2019).** Built on cash equities; its trading illustration is a short-term strategy yielding ~0.23% **before** transaction costs. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327011035_DeepLOB_Deep_Convolutional_Neural_Networks_for_Limit_Order_Books)
- **[EQUITY-MECHANISM] Briola, Bartolucci & Aste, "Deep limit order book forecasting: a microstructural guide" (*Quantitative Finance*, 2025).** A cost-aware review of DeepLOB-type models; notes that forecasting accuracy depends strongly on stock microstructure (tick size and so on), and that limit-order execution and actual fills, not mid-price, determine tradability. — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2025.2522911); [arXiv 2403.09267](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09267)
- **[CRYPTO] Silantyev (2019), "Order flow analysis of cryptocurrency markets", *Digital Finance* 1:191–218.** BitMEX XBTUSD perpetual trade and quote data: **trade flow imbalance (TFI, i.e. aggressor/CVD-type flow) explains contemporaneous price changes better than CKS aggregate OFI** at 1-second to 1-minute aggregation. This is contemporaneous, not predictive. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/digfin/v1y2019i1d10.1007_s42521-019-00007-w.html); [ResearchGate figs (1 s, 1 min)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332089928_Order_flow_analysis_of_cryptocurrency_markets)
- **[CRYPTO] Albers, Cucuringu, Howison & Shestopaloff (2021/22), "Fragmentation, Price Formation, and Cross-Impact in Bitcoin Markets" (arXiv 2108.09750; accepted version at QMUL).** Price formation at **sub-1-second** scales across the most liquid BTC markets, including perps. The target is **~500 ms-ahead returns on the Bybit BTC perpetual**. Transformed (nonlinear) trade-flow-imbalance features give the largest gain; the classical imbalance gets R² ≈ 0.118 (Bybit BTC/USD) and 0.089 (HBDM BTC-USD), versus ≈ 0.165 / 0.130 for the proposed transformed measure. The authors warn that (B−S)/(B+S) is unstable at tiny volumes (a $1 buy vs $10M buy gives the same value). Snippets suggest these R² values are for the forward target but did not make it fully explicit, so verify. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.09750); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2108.09750.html); [QMUL accepted PDF](https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/78460/Shestopaloff%20Fragmentation,%20price%20formation%20and%20cross-impact%20Accepted.pdf?sequence=2&isAllowed=y)
- **[CRYPTO] arXiv 2308.14235 ("An Empirical Analysis on Financial Markets: Insights from the Application of Statistical Physics").** BTC (and LUNA) OFI regressions: R² ≈ 0.094 at a 1-second horizon and ≈ 0.019 at 10 seconds, which the authors call "modest". They contrast this with equity OFI R² of ~0.65–0.70, and a "Momentum Change" measure may be more robust. Whether the horizon is forward-looking is not fully clear from the snippet. — [arXiv 2308.14235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14235)
- **[CRYPTO] arXiv 2602.00776 ("Explainable Patterns in Cryptocurrency Microstructure", 2026).** Gradient-boosting/SHAP on LOB features for BTC, LTC, ETC, ENJ and ROSE (Binance): **OFI, bid-ask spread and VWAP-to-mid deviation dominate feature importance, consistently across assets**. Imbalance's effect is mostly monotone but its marginal impact shrinks at extreme values, and its strength depends on **tick size**. Includes separate taker and maker backtests (numbers not retrieved). Also a spot/futures example (W/USDT) where spot book imbalance correlates 0.94 with the futures mid's position inside the spot spread. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1); [alphaxiv summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.00776); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400370517_Explainable_Patterns_in_Cryptocurrency_Microstructure)
- **[CRYPTO] arXiv 2607.09230 ("When Does Order Flow Matter? State-Dependent L2 Liquidity-State Transitions in Crypto Futures", 2026).** ETH futures: order flow matters more when the book is already stressed (thin liquidity) before the event, so predictability is state-dependent rather than constant. It cites a Binance-futures study finding stable feature importance of book/trade-flow features across assets. — [arXiv 2607.09230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09230)
- **[CRYPTO] Deep learning on crypto LOBs.** "Deep Learning for Digital Asset Limit Order Books" (arXiv 2010.01241; Coinbase BTC spot) reports that temporal CNNs accurately predict short-term BTC price moves from LOB data (cost-adjusted P&L not found in snippets). A 2026 Hawkes+LOB BTC study (*Decisions in Economics and Finance*) **assumes zero transaction costs** and says its profits "should be interpreted as upper bounds… rather than realizable trading profits." — [arXiv 2010.01241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01241); [Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10203-026-00570-z)
- **[CRYPTO, PRACTITIONER] ETHUSD book-imbalance write-up (Towards Data Science).** Imbalance computed over deeper book levels correlates more with future mid moves at p-period-ahead horizons. — [TDS](https://towardsdatascience.com/price-impact-of-order-book-imbalance-in-cryptocurrency-markets-bf39695246f6)

### Inferences
- The CKS ~65% R² is not a trading signal. For a forecaster, the relevant numbers are the crypto *forward* R² values of roughly 2–16% at ≤1 s horizons, which are small. They are also economically tiny, because a 500 ms–1 s BTC return is a fraction of a basis point to a few bp.
- Aggressor-side trade flow (TFI / CVD increments) appears at least as informative as full-book OFI in crypto (Silantyev; Albers et al.). This matters for Freqtrade, which can see public trades but not a full L2 event stream.
- Altcoins: one study (2602.00776) covers small-caps (ENJ, ROSE) and finds the same feature hierarchy. Tick-size dependence means a large-tick alt (relative to price) will show *stronger* imbalance→next-tick predictability, but the spread is also a larger share of the move. No SUI-specific study was found.

### Gaps
- Full per-horizon tables (R² vs horizon) from Albers et al., 2602.00776 and Kolm et al. were not retrievable (arxiv/ssrn PDFs blocked in this session).
- Xu, Gould & Howison (multi-level OFI) was not located by search.
- No SUI-perp-specific study found; no OKX-specific study found.

## Q2. How fast does order-flow predictability decay? Any evidence at ≥1-minute horizons large enough to beat ~0.10% round-trip taker or ~0.04% maker cost?

### Takeaway
Decay is fast: the forward R² for BTC OFI falls ~5× from 1 s to 10 s, and lagged-OFI forecasting power in equities is gone by ~5 minutes. The only ≥1-minute crypto-perp order-imbalance result found (the Binance "quarter-hour effect", 4–12 h horizon) is statistically significant out of sample but economically tiny: ~0.5 bp gross per trade versus ~10 bp taker round trip. An independent practitioner replication called it "Real Pattern, No Trade."

### Cited Findings
- [CRYPTO] BTC OFI R² ≈ 0.094 at 1 s and ≈ 0.019 at 10 s. — [arXiv 2308.14235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14235)
- [EQUITY-MECHANISM] Lagged (cross-asset) OFI forecasting power is "significant over short horizons (up to 5 minutes), but decays rapidly." — [SSRN 3993561](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3993561)
- **[CRYPTO] Kim & Hansen (2026), "The Quarter-Hour Effect: Periodic Algorithmic Trading and Return Predictability in Cryptocurrency Futures" (arXiv 2607.09426).**
  - Data: trade data for six Binance perpetuals, 2021 to Oct 2024.
  - Pattern: volume and volatility bursts at 1-min, 5-min and especially quarter-hour marks, attributed to algorithms (round trade sizes become less common during bursts).
  - Predictability: quarter-hour opening returns are predictable **out of sample**, and **opening order imbalance predicts returns over 4–12 hours**. Finer clock-time frequencies show much weaker effects. The effect is concentrated in the first ~10 seconds after the :00/:15/:30/:45 mark, which has ~26% more trades and ~32% more dollar volume than ordinary 10-second windows. Results survive excluding funding-settlement openings.
  - Sources: [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09426); [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2607.09426)
- **[NET, press + PRACTITIONER] Economic size of the quarter-hour effect.** CryptoSlate's write-up reports that a simple directional rule earned an **average gross 0.51 bp per trade, before fees**, and that at the 4-hour horizon much of the relation came from earlier quarter-hour flow carrying into later boundaries. The Microstructure Lab (Substack) rebuilt the signal from Binance trades through ~2 years past the paper's sample; the post is titled "Real Pattern, No Trade" (body not retrievable). A GitHub issue implementing it notes the result is "in-sample and gross". — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/the-invisible-opening-bell-inside-cryptos-endless-trading-day/); [Microstructure Lab](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/the-quarter-hour-clock-in-crypto); [GitHub issue](https://github.com/s10023/buibui-moon-trader-bot/issues/937)
- [CRYPTO] On BitMEX, trade-flow imbalance fits contemporaneous price changes at 1-minute aggregation (a contemporaneous fit, not a forecast). — [Silantyev 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332089928_Order_flow_analysis_of_cryptocurrency_markets)
- [CRYPTO] Easley, O'Hara, Yang & Zhang, "Microstructure and Market Dynamics in Crypto Markets" (SSRN 4814346, 2024; later *Journal of Financial Markets*), find that microstructure liquidity and price-discovery measures (Roll, Roll impact, VPIN, Kyle's lambda, Amihud) have predictive power for **price dynamics** of 5 major cryptos via ML, stable through crypto winter. Snippets do not show how much of this is direction versus volatility or distribution (see Q4). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4814346); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418126000261)

### Inferences
- **For a 5-second-polling, 1–15-minute-bar bot, the literature points to essentially no exploitable, cost-surviving directional order-flow edge.** The strongest (sub-second) signals have decayed long before a 5 s loop can act. The one ≥1-minute result is ~0.5 bp gross, about 1/20 of a 10 bp taker round trip and about 1/8 of an 8 bp maker round trip (2 bp × 2 sides × 2 trades ≈ 4 bp entry+exit if both legs fill as maker; the user quoted 0.04% maker round trip).
- Rough break-even framing (an inference, not sourced): a taker strategy must forecast >10 bp plus spread plus slippage per round trip. On 1–15-minute bars, an order-flow forecast with out-of-sample R² of ~1% would have to come with very high BTC volatility and selective trading to clear that. No study found demonstrates this.
- Where flow does matter at minute-to-hour scale, the plausible channels are (a) state or volatility regime (Q4), (b) liquidation/funding-driven flows, and (c) periodic algo flow (quarter-hour). Each is small or volatility-related rather than reliably directional.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed crypto study found that reports out-of-sample, **net-of-fees** directional P&L from OFI/book imbalance at 1-minute+ horizons.
- The Microstructure Lab post body (exact cost assumptions and net result) could not be fetched; only its title and framing are known.

## Q3. CVD / taker buy-sell ratio at minute-to-hour horizons; divergence signals

### Takeaway
There is little rigorous evidence that CVD or the taker buy/sell ratio predicts *direction* at minute-to-hour horizons in crypto perps. Academic uses show taker imbalance is mostly **contemporaneous** with price, or that it *responds* to perp mispricing rather than forecasting returns. Hourly order imbalance in BTC options predicts volatility, not returns. No academic study of "CVD divergence" was found; it is practitioner lore.

### Cited Findings
- **[CRYPTO] Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv 2212.06888).** Defines hourly signed **taker imbalance** on Binance as (taker-buy − taker-sell $vol) scaled to [−1, 1]. When perps trade rich to the no-arbitrage benchmark, taker flow tilts toward selling: a one-unit rise in annualized deviation lowers taker imbalance by 1.1 pp (t = −13.3, Newey–West with one week of hourly lags). This shows **flow responds to mispricing (mean-reverting arbitrage flow)**, not that it predicts returns. — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888)
- **[CRYPTO, options] "Net Buying Pressure and the Information in Bitcoin Option Trades" (arXiv 2109.02776).** Hourly volume significantly predicts next-hour returns and realized volatility. **Order imbalance at hourly, daily or 5-day intervals has no comparable return-predictive power but does affect volatility.** — [arXiv 2109.02776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.02776)
- [CRYPTO] Silantyev (2019): trade-flow imbalance (CVD increments) is the better *contemporaneous* explainer of BitMEX XBTUSD price changes. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/digfin/v1y2019i1d10.1007_s42521-019-00007-w.html)
- [CRYPTO] Albers et al.: the raw taker-imbalance ratio (B−S)/(B+S) is fragile at low volume, and nonlinear transforms of trade-flow imbalance help at sub-second horizons. — [arXiv 2108.09750](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.09750)
- [CRYPTO] A Charles University thesis tests imbalance on Binance futures (BTC, ETH, LTC). Results were not visible in snippets. — [CUNI thesis](https://dspace.cuni.cz/bitstream/handle/20.500.11956/200516/120505902.pdf?sequence=1)
- [CRYPTO] Opening order imbalance at quarter-hour marks predicts 4–12 h returns, but only at ~0.5 bp gross per trade (see Q2). — [arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/the-invisible-opening-bell-inside-cryptos-endless-trading-day/)
- Media price commentary routinely cites Binance 1-hour taker buy/sell ratios (e.g. Blockchain.news, Nov 2026). This is anecdotal narrative, not evidence. — [Blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/20261102-price-prediction-price-btc-pinned-below-every-key-average-as)

### Inferences
- In Freqtrade terms, CVD/taker-ratio features built from public trades over 1–15-minute bars mostly re-describe the bar's own return (contemporaneous). Any lagged predictive content is likely to be small, regime-dependent and below cost. It is more promising as a **filter/volatility-regime feature** than as a standalone directional trigger.
- "Divergence" (price up, CVD down) plausibly captures passive absorption (limit sellers absorbing aggressive buys), but no rigorous out-of-sample test of it in crypto perps was found.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or carefully documented study of CVD divergence signals in crypto perps.
- No study on SUI or comparable alt perps for taker-ratio predictability.

## Q4. Informed trading / toxicity (VPIN, Kyle's lambda) in crypto: does it predict volatility rather than direction?

### Takeaway
The evidence, such as it is, ties crypto toxicity and illiquidity measures to **volatility, crash risk and distribution shifts, not to direction**. VPIN's volatility-forecasting ability is itself disputed in the equity literature, which finds no incremental power once volume and volatility are controlled for.

### Cited Findings
- **[CRYPTO] Easley, O'Hara, Yang & Zhang (2024 SSRN; *J. Financial Markets*).** Five major cryptos. Roll and VPIN are high relative to equity/futures markets, with own- and cross-market effects for BTC and ETH Roll/VPIN. ML shows microstructure measures predict "price dynamics", and results are stable through crypto winter. A third-party commentary claims Kyle's lambda has near-zero permutation importance in their tables and that the measures predict *distribution shifts* (volatility, skew) rather than plain direction. That is secondary, so verify. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4814346); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418126000261); [Aligrithm commentary](https://aligrithm.com/crypto-isnt-structurally-alien-roll-vpin-amihud-predict-distribution-shifts/)
- **[CRYPTO] Guo & Liu (2020), "Could VPIN Predict Bitcoin's Crash?", *Journal of Futures and Options* 13.** Coinbase and Bitstamp intraday data 2015–2018: VPIN has significant predictive power for crash risk (NCSKEW/DUVOL), and higher volume alone does not imply higher crash risk. — [NYCU listing](https://imf.nycu.edu.tw/en/30/40101/per1/Could-VPIN-Predict-Bitcoin%E2%80%99s-Crash-61643859)
- **[EQUITY-MECHANISM] Andersen & Bondarenko (2014), *Review of Finance* 19(1):1–54.** S&P 500 futures. VPIN's apparent volatility forecasting stems from bulk-volume-classification errors that rise with volatility. Controlling for current volume and volatility, **VPIN has no incremental predictive power for future volatility.** — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revfin/v19y2015i1p1-54..html); [CREATES working paper](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_43.pdf)
- [CRYPTO, options] Hourly order imbalance in BTC options affects volatility, not returns. — [arXiv 2109.02776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.02776)
- [CRYPTO, PRACTITIONER] A Microstructure Lab cross-exchange analysis reports that higher realized volatility co-occurs with more toxic flow (correlation, not out-of-sample prediction). — [Substack](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/toxic-flow-segmentation-measuring)
- [CRYPTO] State dependence: order flow has larger impact when pre-event L2 liquidity is stressed (ETH futures). — [arXiv 2607.09230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09230)

### Inferences
- For the user, VPIN/lambda/imbalance-magnitude features are best used for **risk sizing, stop placement, or "don't trade now" filters** (volatility/crash regime), not as long/short signals. Bar-based VPIN approximations (TradingView-style) inherit the Andersen–Bondarenko classification critique.

### Gaps
- No crypto study was found that tests VPIN's incremental volatility power after controlling for volume/volatility (the Andersen–Bondarenko test) on perps.
- The "Kitvanitphasu" BTC VPIN paper could not be located.

## Q5. Market making (imbalance-aware quoting) versus directional taker strategies: which is viable at retail latency?

### Takeaway
Imbalance *is* predictive at the touch, and imbalance-aware quoting improves on naive quoting. However, documented Binance backtests with queue-position and latency modelling show passive quoting losing to **adverse selection** at standard maker fees (2 bp). Break-even typically requires rebate-tier fees. Directional taker use of the same signals faces 10 bp+ round trips against sub-bp to ~1 bp signals. Neither is supported as viable for a retail 5 s-loop bot.

### Cited Findings
- **[CRYPTO] "The Market Maker's Dilemma: Navigating the Fill Probability vs. Post-Fill Returns Trade-Off" (arXiv 2502.18625).** Maker orders on the side favoured by imbalance fill with high probability near the front of the queue, but post-fill returns worsen as queue position worsens. **Net-of-fee tables show naive and imbalance-based maker variants all negative.** Maker strategies "do not offer an easy way around" costs. — [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18625)
- **[CRYPTO, PRACTITIONER] Event-driven backtest on real Binance L2 (BTCUSDT, DOGEUSDT; ~3 days) modelling queue position and latency.** Imbalance and Avellaneda–Stoikov quoting with a strict tune/test split: **every variant negative after fees**. Passive quoting loses ~1.2 bp to adverse selection, so a ~1.2 bp maker *rebate* would be needed to break even. Cutting latency from 200 ms to 5 ms barely changed BTCUSDT results (fill selection, not speed, drives the losses). — [GitHub SAIndustries/LOB-Market-Making](https://github.com/SAIndustries/LOB-Market-Making)
- **[CRYPTO, PRACTITIONER] Binance XRPUSDT perp tick-level backtester (latency, queue, fees).** Standalone maker strategy loses ~6.9 bp per round trip, and is still −2.5 bp with a 1 bp maker rebate. — [GitHub sgzju/orderflow-alpha-backtester](https://github.com/sgzju/orderflow-alpha-backtester); see also [lucaspaulien/crypto-market-making](https://github.com/lucaspaulien/crypto-market-making)
- [CRYPTO, PRACTITIONER] The hftbacktest "Market Making with Alpha – Order Book Imbalance" tutorial on Binance Futures assumes a **−0.005% (rebate)** maker fee, "the highest market maker rebate available on Binance Futures". A regular USDT-M account pays +0.02% maker. — [hftbacktest docs](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Market%20Making%20with%20Alpha%20-%20Order%20Book%20Imbalance.html); [HFTENGINE](https://github.com/mirkovicdev/HFTENGINE)
- [CRYPTO] HF market-making with tick-level volume-imbalance signals (sub-second to ~2 s windows) — [arXiv 2306.17179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.17179); 2602.00776 also runs separate taker and maker backtests on LOB features (results not retrieved) — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)
- [EQUITY-MECHANISM] DeepLOB's 0.23% figure is pre-cost. A Hawkes+LOB BTC study explicitly reports upper-bound (zero-cost) profits. Reviews stress that fills rarely occur at the mid. — [DeepLOB](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327011035_DeepLOB_Deep_Convolutional_Neural_Networks_for_Limit_Order_Books); [Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10203-026-00570-z); [Deep LOB trading: Half a second please!](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417422019170)

### Inferences
- **Directional taker on order-flow at 1–15-minute bars:** the signal is too small after decay. Expect cost to dominate (10 bp round trip plus spread versus ≤ ~1 bp documented edges).
- **Imbalance-aware maker quoting:** this is the only setting where the signal clearly helps (it reduces adverse selection), but it needs sub-second requoting, queue management and rebate-tier fees. A Freqtrade 5 s loop with standard 2 bp maker fees fails all three. Practitioner evidence finds it negative even at 5–200 ms latency with non-rebate fees.
- **Realistic retail use:** order-flow/CVD/imbalance as a *conditioning* feature (avoid entries into heavy opposing aggressor flow, use maker entries when book imbalance favours the fill, and scale size down when toxicity/volatility is high), layered on a slower edge (e.g. funding/basis or multi-hour signals). Not as the primary alpha.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, like-for-like comparison of maker vs taker strategies on crypto perps at retail latency (seconds) and retail fee tiers.
- Practitioner backtests use short samples (days). Out-of-regime robustness is unknown.
- No study was found on SUI or similar mid-cap perps (larger relative tick and spread) for maker-strategy viability.
